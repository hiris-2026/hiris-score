Explaining the estimate

What drives this estimate

How each factor moves this patient's 1-year risk away from that of a reference patient with the cohort-average value of every factor. Points are percentage points of 1-year risk and add up exactly to the final estimate (Shapley decomposition); the percentage in brackets is each factor's share of all upward and downward changes. Red raises the risk, green lowers it. Contributions describe the model, not cause and effect.

Clinical considerations

    About the model

    Developed in a prospective cohort of 1,118 patients aged 60 years or older who underwent hip fracture surgery between 2017 and 2025 and were alive on postoperative day 3 (404 deaths during follow-up of up to 8 years). Stage 1 uses sex, BMI, ASA grade ≥III, the age-adjusted Charlson index, the haemoglobin-to-RDW ratio and albumin. Stage 2 adds handgrip strength. When a marker is missing, the calculator uses a model refitted without it (C-index shown with the result). The 95% confidence interval reflects uncertainty in the model estimates, not the spread of outcomes among individual patients. Both are Cox models; risks are counted from postoperative day 3 (POD3) for patients alive on that day.

    Discrimination (C-index, bootstrap-corrected): NHFS 0.70, Stage 1 0.73, Stage 2 0.75. In temporal validation (built on 2017–2020, tested on 2021–2025): 0.70, 0.74 and 0.76. Calibration was good up to 5 years in the development population. Absolute risks may differ in other health systems.

    Risk colours refer to 1-year mortality: green below 10%, amber 10–30%, red above 30%. These bands are for readability only and are not validated treatment thresholds.

    Absolute risks reflect the baseline mortality of the development population. Where overall mortality differs, the baseline risk should be updated before absolute risks are used: in an external cohort from the United States (MIMIC-IV), the model ranked patients well (calibration slope 0.92–0.96) but underestimated absolute 1-year risk about 2.6-fold until the baseline hazard was re-estimated, after which observed and predicted risks agreed (observed-to-expected ratio 1.01–1.02).
